## Notes and residual risks

- **Public Domain Mark vs CC0.** The three Radio Aporee recordings carry the contributor-chosen *Public Domain
  Mark 1.0*, a label rather than a formal CC0 waiver. It is a public-domain dedication suitable for commercial
  use, but if the project needs strict CC0 only, replace `leaves`, `night-insects` and `shore`.
- **Content ID.** Public-domain classical recordings are sometimes wrongly claimed on video platforms. The
  Musopen recordings are widely used; if a streamer gets a claim, the archive.org items above are the evidence.
- **Compositions.** Bach, Schubert, Dvořák: long public domain. Tournier (d. 1951; Sonatine 1924): public domain in
  the EU since 2022 and in the US (published by 1930). The raga is traditional, improvised by the performer.
  *Iwashimizu* was composed in 1904 by Nakao Tozan (d. 1956): public domain in the US and Japan; in the EU, life + 70
  runs to **31 Dec 2026**, so it is fully clear from 1 Jan 2027 (the EU's comparison-of-terms rule for non-EU
  authors very likely clears it already). Ship it then, or swap it, if an EU release falls in December 2026.
- **Recordist vs performer.** The two harp pieces were dedicated CC0 by the recordist (archive.org staff, recording
  the recital for the archive); the performer is named but did not upload them herself. Low risk, but if the
  project wants performer-signed releases only, these are the two to confirm with her.
- **Chopin nocturnes** (Musopen, CC0) were used briefly as stand-ins and are no longer shipped.
- **Not shipped (kept as masters only):** the old `water-flowing.ogg`, `city-hum.ogg`, `crickets.mp3`, the
  Spring Spring and Emmntt music (all CC0, see the old CREDITS.md files). They are not bundled: only
  `assets/audio/` is.
- **Rejected during sourcing** (licence turned out to be CC-BY/BY-SA, generated, or unverifiable): see the
  Phase 8 report, `docs/audio/AUDIO_QA_RESULTS.md`.
